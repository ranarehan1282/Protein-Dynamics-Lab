About the lab

The Protein Dynamics Lab at the National Centre for Bioinformatics, Quaid-i-Azam University, is led by Dr. Rana Rehan Khalid. We combine structural and computational biology to explain how proteins move and interact, and use that understanding for drug discovery in cancer, cardiovascular and immune disorders, and for engineering therapeutic biologics.

Research

Our work connects atomic-level motion to biological function and to therapeutic design.

Structural biology

Protein modelling and dynamics

We study how protein structure, flexibility, and conformational changes shape biological function. Our work combines molecular dynamics simulations, homology and structure modelling, AI-based and inverse protein modelling, and advanced sampling methods such as REST2 replica-exchange and accelerated MD. These approaches help us investigate protein architecture, molecular interactions, and the structural basis of function across diverse biological systems.

Drug discovery

Molecular docking and drug design

We use computational approaches to identify and optimise small molecules with potential biological activity. Our workflow integrates structure- and ligand-based drug design, molecular docking, binding energy calculations, pharmacophore modelling, virtual screening, compound library generation, and QSAR modelling. These methods support the identification of promising compounds and the investigation of their interactions with target proteins across therapeutic research areas.

Immunoinformatics

Computational biologics and immunoinformatics

We apply machine learning, deep learning, and structural modelling to understand and engineer therapeutic proteins and peptides. Our research includes antibody modelling, protein design, and the computational evaluation of interactions between biologics and their molecular targets. These approaches support the rational development of therapeutic candidates and the investigation of immune recognition, antibody binding, and immune checkpoint regulation.

Neuroimmunology

Disease biomarkers and neuro-immunology

We investigate molecular signatures associated with disease by integrating transcriptomic and proteomic data, including RNA-seq, microRNA, and cerebrospinal fluid proteomics. We also study intrinsically disordered proteins and their roles in molecular interactions and cellular processes. By combining computational data analysis with biological interpretation, we aim to identify disease-associated patterns and improve our understanding of the molecular links between inflammation, immunity, and neurodegeneration.
